Why Erin Foster is trending: 'Nobody Wants This' creator responds to Adam Brody's 'free Palestine' comments

Views 0

Trend basis: Google's daily trending searches, United States, observed October 28, 2026, 19:37 KST — the #1 query was 'erin foster'. Facts are drawn from Yahoo Entertainment (October 27), Variety (October 26) and the New York Post (October 25), 2026.

The answer first:

The answer first: Erin Foster, creator of Netflix's 'Nobody Wants This', addressed her star Adam Brody's recent comments supporting "a free Palestine" and calling the Gaza conflict a genocide. Appearing on the October 27 episode of the 'Not Skinny but Not Fat' podcast, Foster said: "It's complicated... while I love Adam, I also disagree with him, and that's OK." Actor Tovah Feldshuh, who plays Brody's onscreen mother, had earlier condemned such statements in a New York Post interview, saying "to accuse the Jewish people of genocide is wild."

Key points

Brody's remarks came in a September interview with GQ, in which he said: "Put me down as thinking it's a genocide. Put me down as wanting a free Palestine." The comments circulated widely after the show's new season premiered, drawing responses from his collaborators.

Foster, whose own conversion to Judaism before her 2019 marriage inspired the series, told listeners the show "has always been and always will be a love letter to Judaism," and addressed Jewish fans directly: "I see you and I hear you and I understand how you feel."

Feldshuh, in her Post interview published October 25, described the genocide characterization as "right out of Hitler's playbook" and said she believes "the perseverance of the existence of the state of Israel is crucial to the well-being of the democratic world."

Why it's trending

Three factors converged. First, timing: the show's third season debuted on October 22, putting its cast in the press cycle. Second, the principal cast is now publicly split on a polarizing issue, with the creator and a co-star both responding within days of each other. Third, the show's premise — a rabbi in an interfaith relationship — makes its actors' positions on Israel and Judaism unusually central to its audience.
