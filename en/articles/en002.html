Full moon tonight: the Hunter's Moon rises on October 25 — when to look east and why Saturn is nearby

The answer first: Yes — tonight, Sunday, October 25, 2026, is a full moon night in the United States. October's full moon is traditionally called the Hunter's Moon, and it rises in the east around sunset tonight, staying visible through the night until it sets in the west around dawn. Saturn sits nearby in the sky, so tonight is a two-for-one sight with the naked eye alone.

Key points

October's full moon is the Hunter's Moon — the full moon that follows September's Harvest Moon. It looks full to the naked eye on the evenings of October 24, 25 and 26, so tonight is right in the middle of the window. Look east around sunset for the moonrise; the moon then crosses the sky overnight. Saturn appears close to the moon over the nights around October 23–25. No telescope is needed, though binoculars make Saturn's colour easier to see.

Why is everyone searching this tonight?

People search "full moon tonight" when the moon is about to be at its most obvious — big, bright and rising near sunset, when it is easy to spot from a porch, a park or a car window. October's full moon also carries a traditional name, the Hunter's Moon, which gives the search a second hook: after the harvest, communities once prepared food for winter, and bright moonlight helped with outdoor work. The name is a tradition, not a different kind of moon — it will not be physically larger or more colourful than other full moons.

Timeline

On the evening of October 23, the nearly full moon began its pass by Saturn. On October 24, the moon looked essentially full in the evening sky. Tonight, October 25, the moon rises in the east around sunset at full brightness and travels across the sky overnight. On October 26 and 27, the waning moon rises later each night, and by October 27–28 it passes near the Pleiades star cluster — a separate sight for telescope users.

Useful information

Pick a spot with a clear, unobstructed view to the east — buildings and trees hide the moon while it is low. The best moment is around sunset, when the moonrise is easiest to catch. You need no equipment: the moon and Saturn are both naked-eye objects. With binoculars, Saturn's golden colour stands out; its rings need a telescope. If tonight is cloudy where you are, try again on October 26 — the moon will still look round.

What to watch next

After this weekend the moon wanes, rising later each night, and darker skies return toward month's end — better for stargazing. The moon's pass near the Pleiades around October 27–28 is the next skywatching date worth noting for telescope owners.
